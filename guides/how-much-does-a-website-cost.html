How much does a website cost for a small business?

“How much is a website?” is a bit like “how much is a van?”. It depends what it needs to do. Here’s what actually drives the price, the costs that are easy to miss, and what we charge, so you can compare quotes properly.

By NEMORA · 29 October 2026 · 5 minute read

What affects the price

  • Number of pages. A one-page site costs less than a site with separate pages for each service, area and project.
  • Custom design or template. A design built around your brand takes longer than dropping your logo into a template, but it’s what makes you look different from everyone else.
  • Features. Quote forms, booking systems, member logins and card payments each add work. A booking or payment system is a different job from a brochure site.
  • Content. If you have your photos and wording ready, it’s quicker. If someone needs to write it for you, that’s extra time.
  • Who builds it. Doing it yourself on a website builder, hiring a freelancer or going to an agency all come with very different prices and levels of support.

The costs people forget

The build is only part of it. A website also needs:

  • A domain name (your .co.uk or .com address), renewed every year
  • Hosting, which keeps the site online
  • An SSL certificate, so it shows as secure
  • Changes and fixes after launch: new prices, photos, opening hours, or something that stops working

Some quotes look cheap because they leave these out. Always ask what happens after the site goes live, and what each change will cost.

Questions to ask any web designer

  • Is hosting included, and what does it cost each month or year?
  • Who owns the domain name?
  • How many changes can I make after launch, and how quickly are they done?
  • Can I update it myself from my phone?
  • Is there a minimum contract, and what happens if I leave?
  • Will it work well on phones?
  • Can I see websites you’ve built for real businesses?
